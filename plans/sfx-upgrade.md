# SFX Upgrade — Tier 1: Professional Synth Engine + Ambient Bed

**Status: In progress** (steps 1–4 implemented — engine, redesigned sounds +
variants, voice-pool playback, ambient bed. Step 5, the on-device mix/tuning
pass, awaits playtest; tune recipe numbers in `scripts/generate-sfx.ts` and
re-run `bun run sfx`.)

> Follow-up to the M3 juice pass. M3 shipped "minimal synth" SFX — single
> oscillator + decay envelope per sound. They read correctly but sound like
> placeholder audio. This pass rebuilds the synthesis engine and playback layer
> so the same procedural pipeline produces production-quality sound, and adds a
> quiet ambient space drone under gameplay. Tier 2 (sourcing pro samples for
> hero sounds) is deliberately deferred until this is judged on device.

## 1. Goal

The game sounds _produced_: every SFX has a layered attack/body/air structure
with baked-in space (reverb tails fitting the void theme), capture/flyby chains
play pentatonic melodies as heat rises, rapid retriggers overlap naturally
instead of cutting each other off, and a barely-audible dark drone glues it all
together. Everything remains generated by `bun run sfx` — no licensed assets,
no new dependencies, total audio payload under ~2.5 MB.

## 2. Scope

**In:**

- Rebuild `scripts/generate-sfx.ts` into a small layered synth engine:
  44.1 kHz output, multi-layer recipes, harmonic partials, detuned oscillator
  pairs, pitch envelopes, filtered noise, soft saturation, simple reverb.
- Redesign all 7 existing sounds (release, capture, graze, perfect, flyby,
  death, zone) as layered recipes.
- Round-robin variants (2–3 per sound) for the rapid-fire sounds.
- Playback upgrades in `src/audio/sfx.ts`: voice pools, pentatonic heat→pitch
  mapping, subtle random pitch jitter on non-pitched sounds.
- Ambient bed: procedurally generated seamless loop, played quietly on home +
  game screens, obeying the existing sound toggle.
- Burn bed _(added after playtest)_: looping fire/wind texture while the heat
  multiplier is active, volume/rate stepping with heat level.

**Out (explicitly):**

- Tier 2: replacing hero sounds with sourced/licensed samples — only if this
  pass disappoints on device.
- Music / melodic soundtrack (the drone is texture, not music).
- UI sounds (button taps, new-best fanfare) — possible later polish.
- Stereo files (mono stays: phone speakers are mono, files half the size).
- Runtime DSP, ducking/sidechain systems, audio middleware, new native deps.
- Separate music/SFX settings toggles — one `soundEnabled` covers both.

## 3. Design decisions

### Keep the procedural pipeline (why not samples now)

Synthesized sound fits the game's clean space aesthetic, carries zero licensing
risk, stays tunable in code, and keeps the binary tiny. The current sounds are
weak because the _synth is primitive_, not because synthesis is the wrong tool.
Prove out a real synth before reaching for sample libraries.

### Synthesis engine upgrades (`scripts/generate-sfx.ts`)

- **44.1 kHz** (up from 22 050) — restores the 8–16 kHz "air" band where
  polish lives. Mono 16-bit WAV stays.
- **Layer model** — every sound is a mix of up to three roles:
  _transient_ (short click/thump attack), _body_ (tonal core), _air_
  (filtered-noise tail). Helpers stay pure `Float32Array → Float32Array`.
- **Richer tonal bodies** — additive partials with per-partial decay rates,
  slightly detuned oscillator pairs (~±6 cents) for width/movement, and pitch
  envelopes (fast exponential swoop into the target pitch) so plucks and
  impacts feel physical.
- **Proper filters** — one-pole/biquad low-pass, high-pass, and band-pass for
  noise shaping, replacing the current single smoothing coefficient.
- **Soft saturation** — `tanh` drive on bodies for harmonic warmth; a final
  soft limiter on every mix so stacked layers can't clip.
- **Space** — a small Schroeder reverb (few comb filters + allpass) baked into
  the tails of death, zone, and perfect. This is the sonic identity of "the
  void": dry sounds are for menus, our events echo into space.

### Musical heat mapping (playback, `src/audio/sfx.ts`)

- Replace linear "2 semitones per heat level" with a **minor-pentatonic step
  table** (semitones `[0, 3, 5, 7, 10, 12, 15, 17, 19]` indexed by heat) for
  the capture playback rate. Chains then play actual melodies; every
  interval sounds intentional. Table lives in `sfx.ts` — it's audio tuning,
  not game feel.
- _(Revised after playtest)_ **Flyby escalates by intensity, not pitch** —
  pitch-mapping the flyby read as a slide whistle on device. Each successive
  planet skipped in a chain plays the fire rush louder (volume 0.55 → 1.0)
  and slightly faster (rate 1.0 → 1.2). Pitch carries no meaning here, so it
  also gets jitter.
- No pitch jitter on the scale-mapped capture (would detune the melody).
  Pooled sounds get **round-robin timbre variants**: 2–3 generated takes per
  sound differing in noise seed / detune phase, cycled per trigger.
- Non-pitched sounds (release, graze, flyby) get ±4 % random `playbackRate`
  jitter per trigger — the classic anti-fatigue trick.

### Voice pools (fix retrigger cutoff)

`seekTo(0)` on a single player kills the previous instance's tail — audible on
flyby chains and graze-into-capture stacks. Rapid-fire sounds (capture, graze,
flyby) get a pool of 2–3 `AudioPlayer`s per variant file, cycled round-robin so
tails ring out. One-shot-at-a-time sounds (release, perfect, death, zone) keep
single players. Total player count stays ~20 — trivial preload cost.

### Ambient bed

- **Generated, not sourced** — keeps the zero-licensing pipeline and makes a
  mathematically seamless loop achievable by construction, sidestepping the
  loop-point problem that plagues sourced ambiences.
- _(Revised after playtest)_ The first version was a dark A1 drone — too dark
  and serious on device. Replaced with a **warm lofi chord pad**: a mellow
  I–vi–IV–V progression in A major (Amaj9 → F#m7 → Dmaj9 → E9sus) on a soft
  EP-style pad (detuned sine pairs + gentle tremolo), sub bass roots, vinyl
  dust crackle, and breathing tape hiss. **No drums**: a beat on a ~32 s loop
  turns grating, imposes a tempo that fights the game's pacing, and synth
  drums from this engine would sound cheap. If a real lofi _beat_ is ever
  wanted, that's a sourcing/licensing task — v2 territory.
- Seam strategy _(revised after playtest)_: a sample-continuous seam is not
  enough — `expo-audio`'s `loop` is **not gapless on iOS in SDK 54** (expo
  issue #42880, fixed only in a later SDK), so the restart hiccup is audible
  no matter how the waveform lines up. The loop is instead authored to _end
  in a breath_: the final V chord resolves to silence 0.5 s before the loop
  point (dust/hiss also reach zero there), and the restart is the I chord
  swelling from silence. The player's gap lands inside an intentional
  musical rest.
- 32 s loop at 22 050 Hz (lofi is band-limited by definition — half the
  bytes), mixed very quiet relative to SFX (~1.4 MB).
- New `src/audio/ambient.ts`: one looping `AudioPlayer` (`loop = true`),
  started after `hydrateAppStore()` at app mount, playing on **both home and
  game screens** (glues the transition; runs under recorded clips). Subscribes
  to `soundEnabled` so the toggle silences it immediately — unlike one-shot
  SFX, a check at trigger time isn't enough for a loop.
- Keeps playing through death — the death boom's reverb tail sitting on the
  pad is the intended effect.

### Burn bed (added after playtest — currently PARKED)

> Status: implemented and wired, but **disabled** (`BURN_ENABLED = false` in
> `src/audio/burn.ts`). Two texture iterations both missed on device (first
> too aggressive, softened cut still not right). The user wants to revisit
> and perfect the sound later — rework `burnRecipe()` in the script, re-run
> `bun run sfx`, and flip the flag. Everything below stays true when enabled.

- A constant burning/wind loop plays **whenever heat ≥ 1** — exactly when the
  comet ball stops being white — and steps louder (volume 0.15 → 0.42) and
  slightly faster per heat level, complementing the per-planet flyby pops.
- Texture _(softened after playtest — first cut was too aggressive)_: warm
  rumble + wide airy wind band, embers barely sprinkled, all under a shallow
  slow flicker (`burn.wav`, 6 s, generated).
- The non-gapless-loop problem can't hide in a musical rest here (the texture
  is constant), so it uses **equal-power dual-player looping**: the file is
  windowed by a half-sine, and two looping players run it offset by half the
  loop — sin² + cos² = 1 keeps summed power constant, and each player's
  restart gap falls where its own envelope is silent.
- Driven from the existing heat reaction in `GameScreen` (which already fires
  on flyby gains, landing cools, and camping decay), with eased volume glides
  (exponential approach; ~250 ms up, ~500 ms down) because heat _drops_ have
  no masking one-shot. Death and screen unmount force it to 0. Lives in
  `src/audio/burn.ts`.

### Per-sound redesign sketches

| Sound   | Recipe direction                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| release | soft "thip": tiny noise transient + down-swooping sine body, dry                                                                                                      |
| capture | warm pluck: pitch-swoop attack, detuned triangle pair body, 3–4 partials, touch of saturation                                                                         |
| graze   | spark: band-passed noise burst + metallic high partial, short shimmer tail                                                                                            |
| perfect | chime: two detuned bell tones (inharmonic partials), small reverb bloom                                                                                               |
| flyby   | fire rush: down-swept wide-band noise + ember crackle + low whoomph, all noise, no tone (revised — the original whoosh-tick with a tonal pip read as a slide whistle) |
| death   | two-stage: sub-drop boom + saturated impact noise, long dark reverb tail                                                                                              |
| zone    | swell: slow-attack detuned pad chord blooming into reverb                                                                                                             |

Exact numbers live in the script as named recipe parameters — they are the
audio equivalent of `constants.ts` and get tuned during the device pass.

## 4. Implementation steps (game stays playable after each)

1. **Engine core** — rewrite `scripts/generate-sfx.ts` internals: 44.1 kHz,
   layer/partial/filter/saturation/reverb/limiter helpers, per-sound recipe
   functions. Regenerate the existing 7 sounds at rough sonic parity to prove
   the pipeline. Touches: `scripts/generate-sfx.ts`, `assets/sfx/`.
2. **Sound redesign** — implement the recipe table above, one sound at a time,
   auditioning via `bun run sfx` + playback in-app. Add round-robin variant
   generation (`capture_1.wav`… etc.). Touches: script, `assets/sfx/`.
3. **Playback layer** — voice pools, variant cycling, pentatonic table, pitch
   jitter in `src/audio/sfx.ts`. Public API (`sfxCapture(kind, heat)` etc.)
   unchanged — `GameScreen.tsx` untouched. Touches: `src/audio/sfx.ts`.
4. **Ambient bed** — drone generation in the script; `src/audio/ambient.ts`;
   start at app mount; subscribe to `soundEnabled`. Touches: script,
   `assets/sfx/`, new `ambient.ts`, `App.tsx`.
5. **Mix + device tuning pass** — balance per-sound gains against each other
   and the drone on a real phone speaker _and_ earbuds; verify stacked events
   (capture+perfect over drone) stay clean.

## 5. Testing & acceptance criteria

- `bun run typecheck` and `bun test` stay green (audio is not unit-tested;
  the pentatonic table is data, not logic).
- `bun run sfx` regenerates all assets deterministically-enough to audition;
  total `assets/sfx/` size under ~2.5 MB.
- On device:
  - every event still instantly identifiable — the redesign must not blur the
    vocabulary M3 established;
  - a 10+ heat chain plays a melody, not a chromatic drift;
  - hammering flyby chains produces overlapping ticks, no machine-gun cutoff;
  - no clipping/crackle when capture + perfect + drone stack;
  - drone loop point inaudible after listening through 3+ loops;
  - sound toggle in settings silences drone and SFX immediately;
  - silent-switch playback and background-music mixing still behave
    (`playsInSilentMode`, `mixWithOthers` unchanged).
- The real test: record a 15-second clip **with sound on** — does the audio
  make the clip better, and would it survive a phone speaker at low volume?
